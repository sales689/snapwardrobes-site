Sliding vs hinged wardrobe doors: how to choose for your Sydney home

Space, access, cost and finish compared, plus the cases where sliding is the wrong call.

Comparison Guide · Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Sliding or hinged is one of the first decisions in any wardrobe project, and it is usually decided by the room, not by taste. The right answer comes from three things: how much floor space you have, how you use the wardrobe, and what the doors will do to the look of the room.

This guide compares the two honestly, including where sliding doors are the wrong call. If you are still deciding between wardrobe types, start with built-in vs walk-in wardrobes.

The short answer

How to choose in 30 seconds

Choose sliding if the room is tight, the opening is wide, or you want a mirror in the room. Choose hinged if you have clear space for the doors to swing and you want to see the whole wardrobe at once. Not sure? Our sliding wardrobe doors page shows the options, and we can talk it through at a free consultation.

Space: the deciding factor

Hinged doors need room to swing, roughly the width of each door. In a bedroom where the wardrobe sits close to the bed, that clearance is often the problem. A door that cannot open fully, or blocks the walkway when it does, gets used less.

Sliding doors travel along a track, so they need no clearance at all. That is why they are common in bedrooms, hallways and anywhere the wardrobe faces a bed or another door.

Access: where hinged wins

With hinged doors, the whole wardrobe opens at once. You see everything, reach everything, and the internal layout can run edge to edge without a door in the way.

With a standard two-door sliding set, one door always covers half the opening. Wider openings with three or four doors help, because you choose which section to open. If you want full access in one go, hinged doors (or a walk-in wardrobe) suit better.

Cost: what usually changes the price

For a wide opening, sliding doors usually cost less than an equivalent run of hinged doors, which need more hinges, more alignment and more doors for the same width. Our sliding doors start from $250 per door including GST. Frame and track, a black frame, mirror or glass, and the size of the opening all move the price.

Hinged doors are usually priced as part of the wardrobe itself. For a broader view of what drives the price, see our custom wardrobe cost guide. We confirm a fixed, itemised price at the free in-home design consultation.

Look and finish

  • Mirror sliding doors: bounce light and make a bedroom feel larger. A practical choice if you need a full-length mirror anyway.
  • Frosted glass: softer and lighter than mirror, with a cleaner look.
  • White melamine: a simple, quiet option that works with most rooms.
  • Hinged doors: available in the full Polytec range, which lets the wardrobe look like furniture rather than a door. See our finishes and colours guide.

When sliding is the wrong choice

Sliding doors are not right for every wardrobe. Avoid them if the opening is narrow and you need full access, if the floor or ceiling is badly out of level and cannot be corrected, or if you want a hand-built furniture look with fully integrated door panels. A good designer will tell you this at the measure, not after the quote.

How to decide

Measure the opening width and the clear space in front of it. If you cannot swing a door fully open without hitting something, sliding is the safer answer. If you have the space and want full visibility, hinged is worth the extra consideration. Our guide to measuring your wardrobe shows what to check.

Our built-in wardrobes are available with either door type, and the internal layout is designed to suit your choice.

Your wardrobe usually isn't the wrong size. It is often just the wrong door for the room.

Frequently asked

Are sliding or hinged wardrobe doors better?

Neither is better in every room. Sliding doors suit bedrooms where floor space is tight because they need no swing clearance. Hinged doors suit rooms with space to spare, where you want to see and reach everything at once. The right choice depends on the room, not the door.

Are sliding wardrobe doors cheaper than hinged?

Often, for wide openings. A sliding door set usually costs less than the equivalent run of hinged doors, which also need hinges, alignment and clearance. Our sliding doors start from $250 per door including GST, and the final price depends on the finish, size and track. We confirm a fixed price at the free in-home design consultation.

Can you only access half the wardrobe with sliding doors?

With a standard two-door set, yes: one door sits in front of the other, so you reach half the opening at a time. Wider openings with three or four doors let you choose which section you open. If you want full access in one go, hinged doors or an open walk-in layout suit better.

How much space do hinged wardrobe doors need?

Enough clearance for each door to swing fully open, which is roughly the width of the door. In a narrow bedroom that can mean the door hits the bed or blocks the walkway. Measure the swing before you commit.

What sliding door finishes are available?

Mirror, frosted glass and white melamine are the most common, with other finishes available. Black frames cost extra. We show you samples at the design consultation so you can judge them in your own light.

Can I change from hinged to sliding doors later?

Usually yes, if the opening is square and the structure suits a track. We measure on site to confirm. It is worth asking at the design stage, because the internal layout can be planned to work with either door type.
