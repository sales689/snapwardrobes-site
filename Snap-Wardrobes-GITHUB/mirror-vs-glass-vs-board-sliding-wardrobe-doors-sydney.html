Mirror vs glass vs board sliding wardrobe doors: what each costs and who it suits

A plain comparison of the three most common sliding door finishes, with price guides and the rooms they suit.

Finish Guide · Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

Once you have decided on sliding doors, the next question is what goes in them. Mirror, frosted glass and white board are the three most common choices in Sydney bedrooms, and each suits a different room.

This guide covers what each looks like in practice, a price guide per door, and the cases where we would steer you away from a finish. If you are still weighing up door types, start with sliding vs hinged wardrobe doors.

The short answer

How to choose in 30 seconds

Mirror for a smaller or darker bedroom, or if you need a full-length mirror anyway. Frosted glass for a lighter, more modern look without reflections. White board or painted door if you do not want to see yourself in the wardrobe, or the style of the home is traditional or Hamptons. Our sliding wardrobe doors page shows the options.

Mirror sliding doors

Mirror is the most common choice, and for good reason. It reflects light, makes a bedroom feel wider, and removes the need for a separate mirror on the wall or the back of a door.

The downsides are real. A bedroom with a bed facing the wardrobe means you see the bed reflected in the doors, which some people dislike. Mirrors also show fingerprints and need regular cleaning. If a mirror will bother you at 6am, choose another finish.

Frosted and white glass

Frosted glass gives a soft, clean face to the wardrobe with no reflections. In a modern renovation, especially with a black frame, it reads as part of the architecture rather than as storage.

Glass costs more than standard mirror, and frameless glass costs more again because of the polished edges and hardware. It also shows marks, so a matte finish suits busy households better than a gloss one.

White melamine and painted board

A solid board door is the quiet option. White melamine is simple, hard wearing and easy to match to the room. For a Hamptons or traditional home, painted Shaker-profile doors give the wardrobe a furniture look that mirror and glass cannot.

The trade-off is light. A large run of solid doors in a small, dark bedroom can make the room feel heavier, so we often pair board doors with a light wall colour or a single mirror panel.

Frames and tracks are from $50 per opening, and a black frame is an add-on per door. Frameless glass costs extra. Wider openings often need fewer doors, which can lower the total.

Framed or frameless?

A framed door sits in a slim aluminium frame. It is robust and the sharper price. Frameless doors use edge-to-edge glass with polished edges, so the lines are cleaner and the cost is higher. For most bedrooms, framed is the practical choice. Frameless is worth it when the wardrobe is a feature wall.

Which finish suits which room

  • Small bedroom with little natural light: mirror.
  • Main bedroom with a bed facing the wardrobe: frosted glass or board.
  • Hallway or entry wardrobe: mirror, so you can check yourself on the way out.
  • Kids' rooms: white melamine. Easy to wipe and no glass.
  • Hamptons or traditional home: painted Shaker-profile.

If your internals are fine and only the doors are tired, we can replace the doors and tracks only. If the layout is the problem, it is usually better to redesign the inside at the same time. See our built-in wardrobes, and our finishes and colours guide for board colours.

The right door finish is usually decided by the light in the room, not by the catalogue.

Frequently asked

Are mirrored wardrobe doors outdated?

Not if they suit the room. Mirror is still the most practical choice for small or dark bedrooms. A black frame and a clean, full-height panel look current. What dates a mirror door is a cheap, heavily bevelled or gold-framed look.

How much do sliding wardrobe doors cost per door in Sydney?

Our sliding doors start from $250 per door including GST for standard mirror. Painted Shaker-profile doors go up to around $650. Glass, frameless doors, a black frame and the size of the opening all move the price. We confirm a fixed price at the free in-home design consultation.

Is frosted glass or mirror better for a small bedroom?

Mirror usually wins in a small bedroom because it reflects light and makes the room feel wider. Frosted glass is lighter than board but does not reflect, so it keeps the room calm rather than bigger.

What is the difference between framed and frameless sliding doors?

Framed doors sit in a slim aluminium frame and are the more robust, lower-cost option. Frameless doors use edge-to-edge glass with polished edges for cleaner lines, and cost more.

Can I replace only the doors on my existing wardrobe?

Yes, if your shelving and drawers still work. We replace the doors and tracks only, custom cut to the opening. We measure on site to confirm it suits.

Do black frames cost extra?

Yes, a black frame is an add-on per door. Many people consider it a small cost for a noticeably more modern look, especially with frosted glass.
