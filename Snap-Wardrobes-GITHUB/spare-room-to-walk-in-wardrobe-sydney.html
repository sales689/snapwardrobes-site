Turning a spare room into a walk-in wardrobe: what to know first

Which layouts fit, how to protect resale, and the small details that decide whether it feels like a dressing room or a storeroom. From the team that measures, designs and installs them.

Design Guide · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

It usually starts the same way. The kids have moved out, the guest room gets used twice a year, and the main bedroom wardrobe is overflowing. Turning that spare room into a walk-in wardrobe is one of the most common jobs we design in Sydney, and one of the most satisfying. It is also easy to get wrong. A spare room has windows, doors and power points in fixed places, and it may be a bedroom you want back one day.

This guide covers what to think about before you start, so the room works well now and doesn't cost you later.

Does your spare room have the space?

Almost every spare bedroom has enough floor area. What matters is the width, because cabinetry eats into it from both sides. The working numbers are simple: hanging needs about 600mm of depth, and you want a clear walkway of at least 900mm.

1.5m+Single run or L-shapeOne person, mostly hanging
2.1m+Galley (both long walls)A couple, split his and hers
3m+U-shapeMost spare bedrooms; lots of drawers and shoes

Many spare bedrooms in Sydney homes are around 3m wide. That suits a U-shape well but is usually too tight for an island. An island squeezed into a narrow room makes every drawer awkward to open. If you're unsure, our guide to measuring your wardrobe space shows what to note before we visit.

The bedroom-count question

This is the one people forget to ask. In family suburbs, bedroom count shapes how a home is listed and how buyers filter their search. A four-bedroom house that becomes a "three plus dressing room" can attract a different pool of buyers. A beautiful walk-in will impress at an inspection, but it doesn't always replace a bedroom on paper.

You don't have to choose. If resale is a few years away, design the room so it can go back to being a bedroom without much fuss:

  • Keep the door and the window. Both matter for the room to still read as a bedroom.
  • Don't move walls. Opening the spare room into the main bedroom is a bigger, costlier change and much harder to undo.
  • Leave room for a bed. A U-shape around the walls keeps the centre clear; a fixed island does not.
  • Keep or plan for a wardrobe wall. One section of the fit-out can be designed to stay as a built-in robe later.

If you plan to sell soon, ask a local agent how bedroom count affects homes in your street. For the broader picture, our article on whether built-in wardrobes add value covers what buyers notice.

The short version

Design around what you own, not the size of the room. Keep the door, the window and the walls where they are. Spend on drawers and layout first; add lighting or an island only if the room is wide enough.

Work with the window, not against it

Spare rooms have a window, and that's mostly a good thing. Natural light makes a dressing room pleasant to use. Two things to plan for:

  • Sun fades fabric. Keep hanging off the wall that gets direct sun, or fit a good blind. Under the window is the ideal spot for a bank of drawers, shoe storage or a seat.
  • Leave the window usable. Cabinetry should stop short of the frame so you can still open it, clean it and fit a blind.

Doors, power points and the small things

These are the details that separate a walk-in that feels designed from one that feels squeezed in:

  • Door swing. A hinged door that opens into the room takes a corner you could otherwise fit out. Sometimes the answer is to hinge it the other way; we'll show you both options.
  • Power points and switches. Note where they are. Drawers and panels need to clear them, and if you want one moved or a power point added for a mirror light, an electrician does that before installation.
  • Lighting. One ceiling light leaves the cabinets in shadow. LED strip lighting inside the hanging sections makes a noticeable difference.
  • Flooring and paint. Decide on these first. We'll confirm at the measure how the cabinetry meets the floor.
  • The old robe. An existing built-in can often be kept, refitted inside, or replaced as part of the new layout.

For the layout itself, our walk-in wardrobe design guide covers aisle widths, drawer heights and the seven mistakes that waste the most space.

Design around what you own

Before thinking about finishes, take stock of your clothes. How much needs long hanging? How many shoes? What do you prefer to fold? Most people have far more short hanging and folded items than they think, and too much long hanging is one of the most common ways space gets wasted. A spare room gives you the wall space to get this right, with double hanging, adjustable shelves and soft-close drawers where you'll use them most.

Your wardrobe usually isn't too small. It was just never designed properly.

Frequently asked

Can any spare bedroom become a walk-in wardrobe?

Most can. Allow about 600mm of depth for hanging plus a walkway of at least 900mm. A room around 2.1m wide can be fitted out on both sides, around 3m suits a U-shape, and an island needs roughly 3.6m. We'll measure and tell you honestly what fits.

Will converting a spare room reduce my home's value?

It can affect how the home is listed if the room no longer reads as a bedroom. Keeping the door, the window and enough floor space for a bed, and avoiding wall changes, keeps it easy to turn back. If you plan to sell soon, ask a local agent how bedroom count affects homes in your street.

How much does a spare room walk-in cost in Sydney?

As a guide, most spare room conversions sit in our Classic package (from $3,990 inc GST) or Premium package (from $8,990 inc GST). An island, 2-Pac painted finishes and lighting take it towards Signature, from $16,990 inc GST. The final price is fixed in writing at your design consultation.

Do I need to remove the window or the existing wardrobe?

No. We design around the window, usually with drawers, shoe storage or a seat beneath it, and keep hanging away from direct sun. An existing built-in robe can often be kept, refitted inside or replaced as part of the new layout.

What should I sort out before the design consultation?

Decide on flooring and paint, note any power points or switches you'd like moved (that needs an electrician), and think about what you actually own: how much long hanging, how many shoes, and what you'd fold into drawers. That shapes the layout more than anything.
