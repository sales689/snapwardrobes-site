Finishes Guide

Wardrobe finishes & colours, explained simply.

Melamine, Polytec colours and woodgrains, matt vs gloss, 2-Pac painted and glass doors — what each one is, what it costs, and how to pick the right look for your room.

Finishes Guide · Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

The finish is what you see and touch every day, so it's worth getting right — but the choices can feel overwhelming. Here's the plain version. Think of finishes in three tiers, from most affordable to most premium, then a few decisions on doors, handles and colour.

The three finish tiers

1. White melamine — the value base. 2. Polytec colours & woodgrains — a step up in warmth and character. 3. 2-Pac painted — the premium, any colour you like. Most of the price difference between wardrobes comes down to which tier you choose and whether you add glass or painted doors.

1. White melamine — the value base

White melamine is a hard-wearing board with a clean, bright surface. It's the most affordable finish and it's what most people choose for wardrobe internals — the shelves, drawers and hanging sections you don't always see. Behind good doors, white melamine internals look crisp and keep the price down. It's practical, timeless and easy to keep clean.

2. Polytec colours & woodgrains

This is where you add warmth and personality without going bespoke. Polytec's decorative range gives you real colours and realistic timber looks on a durable board. Popular matt woodgrains include Nordic Oak, Soft Walnut, Notaio Walnut and Sepia Oak, plus neutrals like Classic White and darker tones such as Char. You can use a colour across the whole wardrobe, or mix a woodgrain with white for a two-tone look.

Woodgrains are the most-requested colour finish we install — they bring the feel of timber at a fraction of the cost of real veneer.

Matt vs gloss

Matt is the current favourite, and for good reason: it hides fingerprints and everyday marks far better than gloss, and it gives a soft, modern finish. Gloss bounces more light around, which can lift a darker room, but it shows every smudge. For wardrobes, most Sydney homeowners go matt.

3. 2-Pac painted — the premium

2-Pac (two-pack) is a sprayed, baked paint finish on MDF that gives a smooth, furniture-grade surface in almost any colour. It's the choice for hinged Shaker doors, a Hamptons look, or matching a specific colour in your home — including your wall or trim colour. It costs more than melamine or Polytec, but nothing else looks quite as finished.

Door finishes (for sliding wardrobes)

If you're doing sliding doors, the door face is its own decision:

  • Mirror — doubles the light and makes a room feel bigger. The most popular.
  • Grey or bronze mirror — a softer, warmer take on mirror.
  • Super white glass — crisp, bright and premium, with no reflection.
  • Frameless mirror or glass — edge-to-edge, minimalist look.
  • Painted Shaker-profile — profiled doors sprayed in any colour, our most premium sliding finish.

Compare them on our sliding doors page.

Handles & hardware

Small detail, big effect. Options run from finger-pull (handleless, clean) and shark-nose edges, through to shaker handles and knobs in brushed brass, matte black or nickel. Handleless suits a modern look; handles suit Hamptons and classic styles. And every drawer is worth putting on soft-close — it's the upgrade people notice most.

How to choose (the quick version)

  • Match the floor, not the walls. Walls get repainted; floors don't. A finish that sits well with your flooring will always look right.
  • Light rooms love pale finishes. In a small or darker bedroom, white or a light woodgrain keeps it feeling open. Save dark tones for larger, brighter rooms.
  • Statement or timeless? If you'll want it to feel current in ten years, lean neutral and add character with handles. If it's a walk-in you'll enjoy every day, a woodgrain or painted colour is worth it.
  • See it in your own light. Finishes look completely different in the showroom vs your bedroom. Always hold a sample against your floor and light before you decide.
The finish sets the mood, but the layout does the work. Get the internals right first, then choose a finish you'll still love in a few years.

Frequently asked

Does a coloured or woodgrain finish cost more than white?

Usually a little. White melamine is the base; Polytec colours and woodgrains step up from there, and 2-Pac painted is the premium. The jump is about the board and door, not the design, so you can have a colour finish without a big price rise.

Matt or gloss — which is better?

Matt is the current favourite. It hides fingerprints and marks far better than gloss and gives a soft, modern look. Gloss reflects more light, which can suit darker rooms, but it shows smudges. For wardrobes, most people choose matt.

Can you match an existing colour in the room?

Often, yes — we bring samples to the consultation so you can hold finishes against your floor, walls and light. With 2-Pac painting we can match almost any colour, including your wall or trim.

What's the most popular finish?

White melamine internals behind clean doors, for value and a bright, timeless look. For warmth, matt woodgrains like Nordic Oak and Soft Walnut are the most requested colour finishes.
