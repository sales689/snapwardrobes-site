Finishes Guide

Wardrobe finishes & colours, explained simply.

Melamine, Polytec colours and woodgrains, matt vs gloss, 2-Pac painted and glass doors — what each one is, what it costs, and how to pick the right look for your room.

Finishes Guide · Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

The finish is what you see and touch every day, so it's worth getting right — but the choices can feel overwhelming. Here's the plain version. Think of finishes in three tiers, from most affordable to most premium, then a few decisions on doors, handles and colour.

The three finish tiers

Most of the price difference between two wardrobes comes down to which tier you choose and whether you add glass or painted doors. Here is how the three compare at a glance.

Tier 1

White melamine

The value base. Bright, hard wearing and easy to keep clean.

Best for: internals, rentals, kids' rooms and tighter budgets.
Cost level: base price.
Tier 2

Polytec colours & woodgrains

A step up in warmth and character, with real colours and timber looks.

You can mix tiers in one wardrobe. White melamine inside with Polytec or painted doors outside is the most common way to get the look without paying premium prices for every surface.

1. White melamine: the value base

White melamine is a hard-wearing board with a clean, bright surface. It is the most affordable finish, and it is what most people choose for wardrobe internals: the shelves, drawers and hanging sections you do not always see. Behind good doors, white melamine internals look crisp and keep the price down.

Why it works so well inside a wardrobe: a white interior reflects light, so you can see clothes at the back of a shelf, and it matches the edging so the cut edges look finished. It also wipes clean, which matters in drawers and shoe shelves.

Choose it when the budget needs to go into the layout, drawers and doors rather than the board colour. Think twice when the wardrobe is a feature of the room and you want it to feel like furniture. In that case, step up to a Polytec colour on the visible surfaces.

2. Polytec colours & woodgrains

This is where you add warmth and personality without going bespoke. Polytec is the board and finish range we work with most. Its decorative range gives you real colours and realistic timber looks on a durable 16mm board. Popular matt woodgrains include Nordic Oak, Soft Walnut, Notaio Walnut and Sepia Oak, plus neutrals like Classic White and darker tones such as Char.

Woodgrains are the most-requested colour finish we install. They bring the feel of timber at a fraction of the cost of real veneer, and the grain hides everyday marks better than a plain flat colour does.

How to use a woodgrain well

  • Mix it with white. A woodgrain on the doors or a feature section, with white inside, gives a two-tone look and keeps the cost sensible.
  • Pick the tone against your floor. A light oak next to dark floorboards can look like a mismatch. Hold the sample on the floor before deciding.
  • Go all-in on walk-ins. A walk-in is a room you stand inside, so a full woodgrain or colour finish is usually worth it there.

Matt vs gloss

Matt is the current favourite, and for good reason: it hides fingerprints and everyday marks far better than gloss, and it gives a soft, modern finish. Gloss bounces more light around, which can lift a darker room, but it shows every smudge. For wardrobes, most Sydney homeowners go matt.

3. 2-Pac painted: the premium

2-Pac (two-pack) is a sprayed, baked paint finish on MDF that gives a smooth, furniture-grade surface in almost any colour. It is the choice for hinged Shaker doors, a Hamptons look, or matching a specific colour in your home, including your wall or trim colour. It costs more than melamine or Polytec, but nothing else looks quite as finished.

Two things worth knowing before you commit. First, paint is a surface, so a hard knock can chip it in a way a through-colour board will not. Second, the colour is chosen from a paint code, so the finish you see on a sample is the finish you get. Bring the paint code from your walls or trim to the consultation if you want a match.

Choose it when the wardrobe or cabinetry is a feature, or when only an exact colour will do. For the same look across a laundry or kitchen, see our laundry cabinets and custom kitchens.

Browse the colour ranges yourself

Looking at real samples online first can save time at the consultation. These are the main Australian ranges you will come across:

  • Polytec: the decorative board range, including Woodmatt woodgrains and plain matt colours.
  • Laminex: boards and laminates in a wide spread of colours and textures.
  • Formica: laminates, often used on benchtops and cabinet faces.
  • Hettich and Blum: drawer and soft-close hardware.

Screens show colour differently, so treat these as a shortlist. If you have fallen for a colour from another range, bring the code to the consultation and we will tell you what is possible.

Door finishes (for sliding wardrobes)

If you're doing sliding doors, the door face is its own decision:

  • Mirror — doubles the light and makes a room feel bigger. The most popular.
  • Grey or bronze mirror — a softer, warmer take on mirror.
  • Super white glass — crisp, bright and premium, with no reflection.
  • Frameless mirror or glass — edge-to-edge, minimalist look.
  • Painted Shaker-profile — profiled doors sprayed in any colour, our most premium sliding finish.

Compare them on our sliding wardrobe doors page, see what each costs per door in our mirror vs glass vs board guide, or read sliding vs hinged doors if you are still choosing a door type.

Handles & hardware

Small detail, big effect. Options run from finger-pull (handleless, clean) and shark-nose edges, through to shaker handles and knobs in brushed brass, matte black or nickel. Handleless suits a modern look; handles suit Hamptons and classic styles. And every drawer is worth putting on soft-close — it's the upgrade people notice most.

How to choose (the quick version)

  • Match the floor, not the walls. Walls get repainted; floors don't. A finish that sits well with your flooring will always look right.
  • Light rooms love pale finishes. In a small or darker bedroom, white or a light woodgrain keeps it feeling open. Save dark tones for larger, brighter rooms.
  • Statement or timeless? If you'll want it to feel current in ten years, lean neutral and add character with handles. If it's a walk-in you'll enjoy every day, a woodgrain or painted colour is worth it.
  • See it in your own light. Finishes look completely different in the showroom vs your bedroom. Always hold a sample against your floor and light before you decide.
The finish sets the mood, but the layout does the work. Get the internals right first, then choose a finish you'll still love in a few years.

Frequently asked

Does a coloured or woodgrain finish cost more than white?

Usually a little. White melamine is the base; Polytec colours and woodgrains step up from there, and 2-Pac painted is the premium. The jump is about the board and door, not the design, so you can have a colour finish without a big price rise.

Matt or gloss — which is better?

Matt is the current favourite. It hides fingerprints and marks far better than gloss and gives a soft, modern look. Gloss reflects more light, which can suit darker rooms, but it shows smudges. For wardrobes, most people choose matt.

Can you match an existing colour in the room?

Often, yes — we bring samples to the consultation so you can hold finishes against your floor, walls and light. With 2-Pac painting we can match almost any colour, including your wall or trim.

What's the most popular finish?

White melamine internals behind clean doors, for value and a bright, timeless look. For warmth, matt woodgrains like Nordic Oak and Soft Walnut are the most requested colour finishes.

What is the difference between melamine and 2-Pac?

Melamine is a coloured board that is the same colour all the way across the surface, hard wearing and the most affordable. 2-Pac is a sprayed, baked paint finish on MDF that can be any colour and gives a smoother, more furniture-like look. 2-Pac costs more and can chip if it takes a hard knock.

Do you work with Polytec, Laminex and Formica?

Polytec is the board and finish range we work with most. If you have chosen a colour from another Australian range, such as Laminex or Formica, bring the code to the free in-home design consultation and we will tell you what is possible.

Can I mix finishes in one wardrobe?

Yes, and it is the most common way to control the price. White melamine inside with a Polytec woodgrain or painted doors outside gives you the look where you see it, without paying premium prices for every surface.

How do I choose a wardrobe colour that will not date?

Lean neutral on the big surfaces and add character with handles or a single feature section. Match the tone to your floor rather than your walls, because walls get repainted and floors usually do not.
